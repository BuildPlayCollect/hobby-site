Sanctioned Magic: The Gathering events follow the **Magic Tournament Rules** (MTR) published by Wizards of the Coast. This page sums up the parts a new tournament player is most likely to need, from the version effective February 27, 2026.

## Deck rules

- **Constructed decks** must contain a minimum of sixty cards. There is no maximum deck size.
- A sideboard is optional and may not contain more than fifteen cards.
- Except for cards with the basic supertype (such as basic lands) or cards whose text says otherwise, a player's combined deck and sideboard may not contain more than four of any individual card, based on its English card title.
- **Limited decks** must contain a minimum of forty cards. There is no maximum deck size.

## Formats

Wizards of the Coast sanctions these formats as individual, three-person team, or Two-Headed Giant tournaments:

- **Constructed:** Standard, Pioneer, Modern, Legacy and Vintage
- **Limited:** Sealed Deck, Booster Draft and Rochester Draft

Events also run at a **Rules Enforcement Level**. Regular tournaments are focused on fun and social aspects, not enforcement. Competitive tournaments are usually those with significant cash prizes or invitations to Professional tournaments. Professional events hold players to a higher standard of technically correct play.

## Match structure and time

- A match is a series of games played until one side has won a set number of games, usually two. Drawn games do not count toward that goal.
- Before each game, players shuffle, present their decks to the opponent for more shuffling, draw seven cards, and may take mulligans in turn order.
- The required minimum time limit for any match is 40 minutes. The recommended round length for Constructed and Limited tournaments is 50 minutes. The recommendation for single-elimination quarterfinal or semifinal matches is 90 minutes, and for single-elimination finals it is no time limit.
- Sealed Deck: 20 minutes for deck registration and 30 minutes for deck construction. Draft: 25 minutes for deck registration and construction.
- **When time runs out:** the player whose turn it is finishes their turn, and five additional turns are played in total (three in team formats such as Two-Headed Giant). If the game is still not finished, it is a draw. Single-elimination matches cannot end in a draw, and the MTR gives life-total tiebreakers for them.

## Sleeves and conduct

- Players may use plastic card sleeves. If they do, all sleeves must be identical and every card must be placed in them the same way.
- Sleeves on double-faced cards must be completely opaque.
- During a match, a player may ask a judge to inspect an opponent's sleeves. The judge can disallow sleeves that are marked, worn or interfere with shuffling or play.
- Section 5 of the MTR covers tournament violations: cheating, bribery, wagering, unsporting conduct, slow play and outside assistance. Cheating will not be tolerated, and stalling is not acceptable. Players must play at a pace that lets the match finish within the announced time limit.

## Penalties

The MTR does not list penalty levels itself. For example, when the Head Judge believes a player has cheated, they issue the appropriate penalty based on the **Infraction Procedure Guide** or the **Judging at Regular Rules Enforcement Level** document. All disqualifications are subject to review, and further penalties can follow.
