Thinking about entering your first Pokémon TCG tournament? Official events run under Play! Pokémon, and the rules are spread across two documents. The **Tournament Rules Handbook** covers every Play! Pokémon game, and the **TCG Tournament Handbook** adds the rules specific to the card game. This page sums up the basics from both. Both documents show a last revision date of May 21, 2026.

## Deck rules

- **Constructed decks** must contain exactly 60 cards.
- A deck may not contain more than 4 copies of a single card, as defined by the card's English language name. Basic Energy cards are the exception.
- Constructed matches are played for 6 Prize cards.
- **Limited decks** (Sealed and draft events) must contain exactly 40 cards and are played for 4 Prize cards. In Limited there is no limit on cards with the same name unless the card's own text says otherwise.
- All Play! Pokémon Championship Series events require players to complete a deck list.
- Proxies may only be used in the specific situation the handbook allows. Otherwise, proxies may not be used at Play! Pokémon tournaments.

## Formats

- **Constructed formats:** Standard, Expanded and Unlimited. Standard and Expanded legality is set by regulation marks and set lists in the handbook. Unlimited decks may contain cards from any Pokémon TCG expansion.
- **Newly released cards** become legal on the second Friday after that product's US market release date.
- **Limited formats:** Sealed, Booster Draft and Build & Battle Draft. In Sealed, players have 30 minutes to build their decks.
- **Age divisions:** Play! Pokémon, where applicable, splits players into three age divisions: Junior, Senior and Masters. The birth years for each division change every season, so check the current handbook.

## Match structure and time

- A match is a game or series of games played against a single opponent. Matches can be a single game, but most are played best-of-three.
- At Championship Series events, Swiss rounds can be single-game or best-of-three at the organizer's choice. Single-elimination rounds in events that use Swiss plus single elimination must be best-of-three.
- Championship Series rounds must be given at least **30 minutes** for a single game and at least **50 minutes** for best-of-three.
- **When time is called:** games in progress do not end right away. Players have 15 additional minutes from when the timer hits 0 to complete an additional three turns (+3 turns). When those 15 minutes are up, the round ends.
- In Swiss rounds a game can end in a tie. In single elimination a winner is required, and unresolved games go to a tiebreaker game.

## Sleeves and conduct

- Each sleeve must hold a single card in its entirety, and all sleeves in a deck must be the same color or design, condition, size and texture.
- Sleeve edges must be a single solid color, and sleeves must not be so reflective that they reveal card faces.
- The handbook strongly recommends sleeves with an opaque back.
- Players are expected to follow the direction of tournament staff, be respectful and courteous to all, avoid inappropriate language and inflammatory topics, and be free from the influence of mind-altering substances, including alcohol.

## Penalties

The Tournament Rules Handbook lists these penalties. The separate Play! Pokémon Penalty Guidelines take priority over this summary.

- **Caution:** the most basic penalty. It is verbal and not reported to Play! Pokémon.
- **Warning:** a reported penalty for a minor issue.
- **Double Prize Card Penalty and Quadruple Prize Card Penalty** (TCG only): a competitor needs to take two or four fewer Prize cards to win their game.
- **Game Loss:** used when a mistake severely affects the integrity of the game state.
- **Disqualification:** the most severe penalty. The player is removed from the standings and cannot receive prizes.
