Two tops. One stadium. A countdown, a rip, and about a minute of pure chaos.

Beyblade feels like a modern invention, but its roots go back centuries, to a toy that started life as a seashell full of sand. Grab your launcher. Here's how we got from there to a stadium near you.

## Before Beyblade: the beigoma

Long before anyone yelled "Let it rip!", people in Japan were battling with [**beigoma**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beigoma), small traditional tops about 3 cm wide. The earliest ones were made by filling spiral seashells called *bai* with sand and sealing them with wax. Later versions were made of lead, then cast metal.

📷 PHOTO: A beigoma top. If you don't own one, I'll find a free public domain photo.

The game was simple: stretch a canvas sheet over a bucket, launch your top with a cord, and win by spinning longest or knocking your rival out. Beigoma were well known by the Edo period, and their popularity peaked in the first half of the 20th century.

Sound familiar? [Beyblades were inspired by them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyblade).

## Two misses, then a hit

Beyblade wasn't Takara's first swing at battling tops. Its predecessor released "Suge Goma" in 1995 and "Battle Top" in 1997, and [neither took off](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/entertainment/beyblade-25th-anniversary/). Third time's the charm.

## Generation 1: the original Beyblade (1999 to 2004)

The first Beyblades [launched in Japan in **1999**](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202503105477). That same year, Takao Aoki's [*Bakuten Shoot Beyblade* manga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyblade_(manga)) began in *CoroCoro Comic* on August 10, 1999, running until 2004.

📷 PHOTO: An original plastic-generation Beyblade (1999 to 2004), if you or a friend still has one.

The anime [followed on TV Tokyo in January 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyblade_(manga)), and reached ABC Family in the US in 2002. Suddenly every kid knew [Tyson's Dragoon, Kai's Dranzer, Ray's Drigger and Max's Draciel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Beyblade_characters).

These tops were made mostly of plastic. Hasbro took them international in 2002, and by 2003 more than 5 million had sold in the US alone. In the UK, Beyblade was [the most sought-after toy for Christmas 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyblade).

## Generation 2: Metal Fight Beyblade (2008 onward)

Takara merged with Tomy in 2006, and two years later the new Takara Tomy released **Metal Fight Beyblade** on July 12, 2008. As the name promises, this generation [added metal parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyblade).

📷 PHOTO: A Metal Fight Beyblade, showing its metal parts.

Known in English as *Beyblade: Metal Fusion*, its anime ran across [four seasons from 2009 to 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_Fight_Beyblade): Metal Fusion, Metal Masters, Metal Fury and Shogun Steel. In 2011, Beyblades were [the top-selling battling toys in the US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyblade).

## Generation 3: Beyblade Burst (2015 onward)

In 2015, **Beyblade Burst** gave tops a dramatic new trick: take enough hits and your Bey can [*burst* apart mid-battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyblade). Fun fact: that idea [came from a colleague in a different department](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/entertainment/beyblade-25th-anniversary/) at the company.

📷 PHOTO: A Beyblade Burst top, ideally mid-burst with the pieces flying apart.

Hasbro started selling Burst in the US in January 2017. The anime was a marathon, running [seven seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyblade_Burst) from Burst in 2016 to QuadStrike, which ended in December 2023.

## Generation 4: Beyblade X (2023 to today)

**Beyblade X** [launched in Japan on July 15, 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyblade_X). Its big idea is [**Xtreme Dash**](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/entertainment/beyblade-25th-anniversary/), which speeds tops up so they crash into each other more often, helped by the Xtreme Rail built into the stadium. Battles were also shortened to about a minute.

📷 PHOTO: A Beyblade X top, or a battle in an Xtreme stadium showing the rail.

It worked. Beyblade X shipped [more than 28 million units worldwide by February 2025](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202503105477). The anime started in October 2023, and its [fourth season premiered on October 9, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyblade_X).

## Rare tops collectors chase

Every generation has its grails, and Beyblade X is no exception. Its rarest tops usually carry a "00" code (BX-00, UX-00) and never hit regular store shelves. We keep a list of them in our [Beyblade X parts list](/beyblade/parts/). Three favorites:

📷 PHOTO: Aero Pegasus, gold Wizard Rod and navy Phoenix Wing together (only the ones you own, or a friend's with permission).

- **Aero Pegasus 3-70A.** For more than two years, the only way to get one was through the Beyblade X app's "Rare Bey Get Battle". Players needed a Rare Bey Exchange Ticket, which app users had [a 0.7% chance](https://beyblade.fandom.com/wiki/AeroPegasus_3-70A) of getting, then redeemed it for this [UX-00 prize, first released in Japan on July 30, 2024](https://beyblade.fandom.com/wiki/Blade_-_AeroPegasus). Plot twist: on October 10, 2026, it finally [came to stores in Japan in the BX-53 All-in-One Set](https://www.toy-people.com/en/?p=114407), in a dual metallic blue and aqua finish. Good news for players, slightly less good news for collectors' bragging rights.
- **Wizard Rod (Metal Coat: Gold).** You won't find this one in any store. It's a [UX-00 G3 tournament prize](https://beyblade.fandom.com/wiki/Blade_-_WizardRod), and G3 events are among the [higher-level tournaments run by official B4 stores](https://www.takaratomyasia.com/documents/beybladex/1705650582_Beyblade%20X_Start%20Dash%20Guide.pdf). Want a gold Bey? Go win it.
- **Phoenix Wing 9-80DB (Metal Coat: Navy).** A CoroCoro Comic special, offered through the magazine's [October issue as a paid mail-order for every reader who applied](https://snkrdunk.com/en/streetwears/389713). Easy to get at the time, if you were reading CoroCoro that month. Miss the window, and you're shopping resale.

The nostalgia runs deep, too. Rare Beyblade X releases include tops named after the original four bit-beasts: **Dranzer Spiral**, **Driger S**, **Draciel Shield** and **Dragoon Storm**. The 1999 kids are all grown up, and they're buying.

## From toy to sport

The way people play has changed as much as the tops. What started as backyard battles on a bucket is now a sport with world championships.

📷 PHOTO: A local tournament: the stadium, the crowd or a launch. Ask people before showing their faces.

- **More grown-ups.** Adult fans grew during the second and third generations, which Takara Tomy [credits to social media](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/entertainment/beyblade-25th-anniversary/). The company's goal is for Beyblade to become a sport that people of every age and culture can enjoy.
- **World stages.** The [Beyblade X World Championship 2025](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202503105477) was held at Red Tokyo Tower on October 11 and 12, 2025. In 2026, there's even a [world championship on Roblox](https://www.takaratomy.co.jp/english/product_release/pdf/p260321_en.pdf) (May 15 to 25), and the Beyblade X GP Final heads to Bangkok on December 12 and 13. That's a short flight from Manila.

## How big is Beyblade, really?

[Over **560 million units** shipped](https://www.takaratomy.co.jp/english/product_release/pdf/p260321_en.pdf) in more than 80 countries and regions, as of September 2025. If you lined them up... actually, please don't. Just battle them.

From a seashell full of sand to a rail-riding Bey, the idea never changed: two tops, one winner, and a crowd that can't look away. Whether you grew up with Dranzer or just bought your first Beyblade X starter, you're part of a story that's centuries old.
