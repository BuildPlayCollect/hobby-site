Every keyboard has a voice. Some go *thock* like a wooden block. Some go *clack* like a tiny typewriter. Some sound like someone is popping bubble wrap made of marbles.

People in the hobby have a word for each of them. Here's what those words mean, and how to build the sound you want without guessing.

## First, a warning

These words are [community slang, not technical terms](https://www.slashgear.com/1889435/creamy-keyboard-explained/). Nobody measures "creaminess" in a lab. And most guides are written by shops that sell the parts they describe. So treat everything below as a map, not a law. Your ears get the final vote.

## The sounds, in plain words

**Thock.** Deep, low and full, with very little ring. [Thocky doesn't have to mean loud](https://rkgamingstore.com/blogs/community/keyboard-sound-profiles-thocky-creamy-clacky), it just has more body.

**Clack.** Bright and crisp. Each keystroke stands out, with a higher pitch and a bit of a "plasticky" edge.

**Creamy.** Smooth and soft, with [no scratch and no metallic ping](https://www.monsgeek.com/blog/what-is-creamy-keyboard/). Creamy is about smoothness, not pitch, so a board [can be creamy and thocky, or creamy and clacky](https://mkbguide.com/blog/keyboard-sound-thock-clack-guide).

**Marbly.** Usually a round sound with a soft bounce, like a [marble rolling around a bowl](https://epomaker.com/pages/sound-lab). Some guides use it for an extremely deep, short thock instead.

**Poppy.** Usually a [bright little snap at the end of each press](https://epomaker.com/pages/sound-poppy). Others use it for the sound a tactile switch makes at its bump. Ask two people and you might get two answers.

**Muted.** As quiet as a mechanical keyboard gets: silent switches plus lots of foam.

## What actually makes the sound

Think of a keyboard as a stack. Each layer changes the voice a little.

**The case.** Aluminum sounds [crisp and bright, and can "ping"](https://keebsforall.com/blogs/mechanical-keyboards-101/how-case-material-changes-keyboard-sound) without damping. Polycarbonate sounds warmer and more muted. Cheap plastic tends to sound higher and hollow. Brass weights inside a case can [add a fuller low end](https://keebsforall.com/blogs/mechanical-keyboards-101/frame-material-affects-typing-sound-feel). Many builders argue that mass and mounting matter more than the material itself.

**The plate.** Softer plates sound lower. One shop ranks them [from lowest to highest pitch](https://lumekeebs.com/blogs/blog/plate-materials-does-it-make-a-difference): PP, PC, POM, FR4, aluminum, brass, carbon fiber, copper, steel. Guides disagree about brass and carbon fiber, so take the middle of that list loosely.

**The mount.** This is how the plate sits in the case. A [gasket mount](https://akkogear.eu/blogs/news/keyboard-mounting-style) cushions the plate for a softer, more even sound. A tray mount screws everything to the bottom and is often linked with clackier builds.

**The switches.** Housing plastics get a lot of hype, but one reviewer ran a listening test and [couldn't predict a switch's sound from its material](https://www.digitec.ch/en/page/keycap-kevin-not-all-plastics-are-created-equal-and-its-the-same-story-for-switch-housing-26510). What clearly helps is lube: it cuts scratch, and lube is the [only real cure for spring ping](https://www.tomshardware.com/uk/features/mechanical-keyboard-tuning-guide).

**The keycaps.** Here's the big one for most people. [ABS caps sound higher and clackier. PBT sounds deeper.](https://www.tomshardware.com/uk/features/mechanical-keyboard-tuning-guide) POM sounds deep and muted. Thickness matters too: Tom's Hardware suggests walls of 1.3 to 1.5 mm and says to avoid caps 1 mm or thinner. Tall profiles like SA tend to sound fuller, while Cherry profile sounds tighter. (Keycap profiles get their own article next.)

## The mods, and when to use them

Start with the one mod nearly every guide agrees on: [lube and tune your stabilizers](https://kineticlabs.com/blog/overview-mechanical-keyboard-mods). Those are the wires under your spacebar and other long keys. Untuned, they rattle, and no amount of foam hides it.

**Tape mod.** Two or three layers of painter's tape on the back of the PCB. It was [popularized by a YouTuber called Tempest in 2021](https://switchandclick.com/how-to-tempest-tape-mod-your-keyboard/), and it makes a board less hollow and poppier. It's cheap and reversible. Two cautions: Switch and Click warns not to tape-mod boards with batteries, and peel the tape slowly so you don't pull off a hot-swap socket.

**Foam mod.** Foam fills the empty space in the case to cut hollowness and echo. Case foam sits at the bottom. Plate foam sits between the plate and PCB to soften the highs. Denser foams like [Poron and EVA give a deeper, more muted tone](https://keebsforall.com/blogs/mechanical-keyboards-101/install-keyboard-foam-guide).

**PE foam mod.** A thin sheet of polyethylene between the switches and the PCB. It gives a [poppy, marbly sound with a thick bottom-out](https://switchandclick.com/pe-foam-mod/). It's harder to undo, so try it during a rebuild.

**Foamless.** Some people pull every bit of foam out. The usual reason, by community word of mouth, is that foam can flatten a board, and foamless keeps more of its natural, lively sound. One modding guide also warns that [stacking more than three or four damping layers can make a board sound "dead"](https://mkbguide.com/blog/advanced-keyboard-modding-techniques-guide).

**O-rings.** Little rubber rings on the keycap stems. They [turn "clack" into "thud"](https://attackshark.com/blogs/knowledges/o-rings-reduce-keyboard-noise-keep-feel), but can feel mushy. The easiest mod of all.

**Force break mod.** A thin pad where the two halves of a metal case touch, to stop case ping. It [does nothing on plastic boards](https://mkbguide.com/blog/advanced-keyboard-modding-techniques-guide).

**Press 'N Seal mod.** Keyboard YouTuber [Hipyo Tech is credited with this one](https://www.howtogeek.com/mods-to-improve-your-mechanical-keyboard/): wrap the PCB in a layer of Press 'N Seal food wrap on each side for extra thock and pop. Cheap, but fiddly.

## Recipes

These come from shop guides and community experience. Every board reacts differently, so change one thing at a time.

| Sound | Case and mount | Keycaps | Mods |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thock | Gasket mount, PC or FR4 plate | Thick PBT | Case foam, tape mod, lubed switches |
| Clack | Tray or top mount, stiff plate | ABS | Little or no foam |
| Creamy | Gasket or plastic case | Thick PBT | Lubed switches and stabs, plate foam |
| Marbly | Gasket mount, PC plate | PBT | PE foam mod, little bottom foam |
| Poppy | Compact 60–65% case | PBT or ABS | Tape mod, thin plate foam only |
| Muted | Any | PBT or POM | Silent switches, full foam, O-rings, desk mat |

The [Epomaker Sound Lab](https://epomaker.com/pages/sound-lab) lists full specs for each of these if you want to go deeper.

## What's on my desk

I'm still training my ears, so these are first impressions, not lab results. Here's how my six boards sound to me right now:

- **Lucky65 v1:** clacky and poppy. HMX Cloud linears, Cherry-profile keycaps, tape mod.
- **PMO Aurora 80:** the thocky one. Keygeek Y2 linears, Cherry-profile keycaps, tape and foam mod.
- **Attack Shark X86:** a bit muted, with a bit of pop. KTT Kang White linears (lubed), Cherry-profile keycaps, tape and foam mod.
- **Tofu60:** marbly. Vertex V1 linears, Cherry-profile keycaps, tape and foam mod, no weight plate.
- **Nut65:** poppy and muted. Kailh Lunar linears, Cherry-profile keycaps, tape and foam mod.
- **Akko MOD005:** poppy. Akko V3 Pro Cream Black linears, tape mod, and the only foamless board of the bunch.

Same keycap profile on all six, and still six different voices. That's the whole stack at work.

## Where to start

Lube your stabilizers. Try the tape mod. Swap keycaps if you can borrow a set. Then listen. The best sound is the one that makes you want to keep typing.
