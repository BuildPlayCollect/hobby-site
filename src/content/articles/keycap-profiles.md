Swap the keycaps on a keyboard and it can feel like a brand new board. Same switches, same case, but a different shape under your fingers and a different sound in your ears.

That shape is called the **profile**. Here's how to tell them apart without a ruler.

## Sculpted or uniform?

Every profile falls into one of two camps.

**Sculpted** profiles give each row its own height and angle, so the keyboard curves toward your fingers like a shallow bowl. **Uniform** profiles use the same shape on every row, so any cap can go anywhere. That's handy for odd layouts and for rearranging your keys.

Heights in millimetres vary wildly between shop guides, so we're sticking to "taller" and "lower" here. One vendor guide even listed a cap height that turned out to be the [width of a key](https://hirosarts.com/blog/guide-to-different-keycap-profiles/).

## The common profiles

**Cherry.** The low, sculpted shape from Cherry's own keyboards. It's [about 1 to 2 mm shorter than OEM](https://akkogear.eu/blogs/news/cherry-keycaps-what-you-need-to-know) and is the profile of most enthusiast sets, including GMK. Familiar, comfy and a great first pick.

**OEM.** The [most common profile](https://epomaker.com/blogs/guides/keycaps-profiles-overview-what-are-they-and-how-to-choose), found on most store-bought keyboards. Sculpted, a little taller than Cherry.

**SA.** Signature Plastics' tall, sculpted profile with round, dished tops. It was [first made for keyboards before 1980](https://deskthority.net/wiki/Signature_Plastics_SA_family) and still screams retro. It's the [tallest of the common profiles](https://keebsforall.com/blogs/mechanical-keyboards-101/keycap-profiles-materials-buying-guide), so expect a learning curve and maybe a wrist rest.

**MT3.** Tall and deeply dished, [designed by Matt3o and sold through Massdrop in 2017](https://mail.deskthority.net/wiki/MT3_Profile), inspired by IBM's old beam spring keyboards. Your fingertips sink right in.

**DSA.** Signature Plastics' low, uniform profile with round tops. It's [7.39 mm tall, the same as an OEM home row](https://deskthority.net/wiki/Signature_Plastics_DSA_family).

**XDA.** Uniform with a wide, nearly flat top, [about 1 mm taller than DSA](https://akkogear.eu/blogs/news/xda-keycaps-overview). Easy to hit, easy to rearrange.

**KAT and KAM.** Two profiles from the maker Keyreative. [KAT is sculpted and moderately tall](https://cannonkeys.com/collections/kat). [KAM keeps the same slope on every row](https://green-keys.info/en/kam-playground-is-now-available-from-keyreative%ef%bd%9cdesigned-by-biip/).

**ASA.** [Akko's own take on SA](https://monsgeek.eu/blogs/guide/keycap-profiles-101-find-the-right-fit-for-you), launched in 2021. Taller than Cherry and OEM, lower than SA.

You'll also spot names like MDA, OSA and MOA. Shop guides even [disagree on whether OSA is sculpted or uniform](https://hirosarts.com/blog/guide-to-different-keycap-profiles/), so try before you buy.

**Low-profile boards** are their own world. Kailh Choc V1 switches use a two-prong stem, so [regular MX keycaps won't fit them](https://blog.keeb.io/low-profile-keycap-options-what-works-with-what/).

## How they sound

Shape changes sound. In general, [taller caps sound deeper and lower caps sound higher](https://protesilaos.com/keeb/2024-04-27-introduction-to-keycaps-for-mechanical-keyboards/). But wall thickness matters too, and a tall cap with thin walls can sound hollow.

One shop describes [Cherry as "sharper", OEM as "balanced", SA as "deep, full" and MT3 as deeper still](https://keebsforall.com/blogs/mechanical-keyboards-101/keycap-profiles-materials-buying-guide). Treat those as rough descriptions, not measurements.

## Which plastic?

| Material | Feel | Sound | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| ABS | Smooth | Higher, clackier | Develops shine from finger oils |
| PBT | Matte, grippy | Deeper | Usually softer, opaque colours |
| POM | Waxy, slick | Deep and muted | Less common, often blank |
| PC | Clear or glossy | Crisp | Can be brittle |

Sources: [KeebsForAll](https://keebsforall.com/blogs/mechanical-keyboards-101/keycap-materials-guide-beginners) and [Voltcave](https://voltcave.com/keycap-types/). The sound column is community experience.

Don't judge a set by plastic alone. How it's made and [how thick the walls are](https://protesilaos.com/keeb/2024-04-27-introduction-to-keycaps-for-mechanical-keyboards/) matter just as much.

## How the letters get on

- **Doubleshot:** the legend is a second piece of plastic molded right through the cap, so it [never wears off](https://akkogear.de/en/blogs/news-en/dye-sublimation-vs-double-shot-keycaps).
- **Dye-sublimation:** ink is heat-pressed into PBT. Great for detailed art, with slightly softer edges.
- **Pad printing:** ink stamped on top. Cheapest, and [it rubs off](https://voltcave.com/keycap-types/).
- **Laser etching:** legends burned in. Can fade or collect grime.

## Matching caps to your sound

If you read our guide to sound profiles, here's the short version for keycaps. Chasing **thock** or **creamy**? Reach for thick PBT or POM. Want **clack**? ABS or PC caps will get you there. And whatever you pick, wall thickness counts: Tom's Hardware suggests [1.3 to 1.5 mm walls and avoiding caps 1 mm or thinner](https://www.tomshardware.com/uk/features/mechanical-keyboard-tuning-guide), because thin caps tend to sound hollow and rattly.

## A quick word on GMK and clones

GMK is a German company, [founded in 1992](https://deskthority.net/wiki/GMK), that took over Cherry's doubleshot tooling. Its sets are [1.5 mm thick doubleshot ABS in Cherry profile, made in Germany](https://www.gmk.net/shop/en/gmk-cyl-motif/gmk10027), usually sold through group buys with long waits.

That wait, and the price, is why "clone" sets exist. They copy a GMK set's colours and often its name, and are usually sold as PBT. The catch, as The Verge reported, is that they come from [an unrelated company with no input from the designer](https://it.slashdot.org/story/22/02/08/1456214/), which can take sales away from the person who created the design. Worth knowing before you buy.

For what it's worth, all six keyboards on my desk wear Cherry profile. It's the one I keep coming back to.

## Where to start

If you're not sure, start with Cherry or OEM. If you want something you'll notice the moment you touch it, borrow an SA or MT3 set. And if you rearrange your keys a lot, go uniform.
