The first big choice in keyboards isn't the switch or the keycaps. It's the size.

Go big and every key has its own home. Go small and you get a clean desk, more room for your mouse, and a few secret key combos to learn. Here's the whole family, biggest to smallest.

## The sizes at a glance

| Layout | About how many keys | What's missing | Great for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full size (100%) | 104–108 | Nothing | Spreadsheets, data entry |
| 96% / 1800 | 96–99 | Nothing, just squeezed | Numpad lovers with less desk |
| TKL (80%) | 87–88 | Number pad | Gaming, coding, everyday use |
| 75% | 80–84 | Nav keys squished into a column | A bit of everything |
| 65% | 66–68 | Function row | Gaming, minimal desks |
| 60% | 60–62 | Function row, arrows, nav keys | Travel, tiny desks |
| 40% | 40–50 | The number row too | Brave enthusiasts |

Key counts and suggestions are from [kbd.news's guide to keyboard sizes](https://kbd.news/Keyboard-Form-Factors-Explained-A-Complete-Guide-to-Keyboard-Sizes-115.html). The "great for" column is a starting point, not a rule.

## Full size

The classic office keyboard, number pad and all. There's [no learning curve](https://kbd.news/Keyboard-Form-Factors-Explained-A-Complete-Guide-to-Keyboard-Sizes-115.html), but it takes up the most space. If you type numbers all day, this is your friend.

## 96% (also called 1800)

All the keys of a full-size board, packed tighter with the gaps squeezed out. You keep the number pad and win back a bit of desk.

## TKL, or tenkeyless (80%)

Take a full-size board and chop off the number pad. That's it. Your mouse moves closer to your keyboard, which is why gamers love it, and everything else stays exactly where you expect it.

![The right end of a red TKL keyboard: arrow keys in their own little block, next to a cluster of navigation keys and Print Screen](/images/articles/keyboard-layouts/tkl-nav-cluster.webp)
*No number pad, but the arrows and navigation keys keep their own spots. Photo: Echo*

## 75%

The function row stays, but the navigation keys (Home, End, Page Up and friends) get squeezed into a single column on the right. It feels compact without losing much. One thing to know: [75% layouts vary between brands](https://kbd.news/Keyboard-Form-Factors-Explained-A-Complete-Guide-to-Keyboard-Sizes-115.html), so the right column can differ from board to board.

## Wait, what's a layer?

Small boards don't delete keys, they hide them. Hold the **Fn** key and other keys change job: the number row becomes F1 to F12, or I-J-K-L becomes your arrow keys. Each "hold this to change everything" mode is a layer. That's the trade: a little memory work for a lot of desk space.

## 65%

Now the function row goes too, but you keep your arrow keys and a few nav keys. F1 to F12 move onto a **layer**: hold the Fn key and the number row becomes your function row. It sounds fiddly and becomes second nature in about a week.

![A teal 65% keyboard up close: red arrow keys tucked under Shift, with Page Up and Page Down in a column on the right](/images/articles/keyboard-layouts/65-percent-arrows.webp)
*A 65%: no function row, but the arrows and Page Up/Down are still there. Photo: Echo*

## 60%

No arrows, no nav keys, no function row. Everything lives on layers. It's [great for travel and small desks](https://kbd.news/Keyboard-Form-Factors-Explained-A-Complete-Guide-to-Keyboard-Sizes-115.html), and it's a favorite for custom builds.

![A purple 60% keyboard with white and cream keycaps on a stone table, a few spare keycaps scattered around it](/images/articles/keyboard-layouts/purple-60-percent.webp)
*A 60%: just the letters, numbers and mods. Photo: Echo*

A famous cousin is the **HHKB**, short for Happy Hacking Keyboard. It was first released by PFU in Japan [in December 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Hacking_Keyboard), has 60 keys, and puts Control where Caps Lock usually sits, which is very handy if you use a lot of keyboard shortcuts. Since the HHKB Professional in 2003, it has used [Topre capacitive switches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Hacking_Keyboard) instead of regular mechanical ones.

![A dark keyboard in the HHKB layout seen from above, with the two blank gaps on either side of the bottom row](/images/articles/keyboard-layouts/hhkb-layout.webp)
*The HHKB layout: see the empty gaps at both ends of the bottom row? Photo: Echo*

## 40%

The number row disappears too. Even numbers live on a layer. It has a [steep learning curve](https://kbd.news/Keyboard-Form-Factors-Explained-A-Complete-Guide-to-Keyboard-Sizes-115.html) and a very loyal fan club. Try one after you've lived with a 60%.

## The fun shapes

**Alice.** Named after the [TGR Alice by designer Yuk Tsi](https://deskthority.net/wiki/Alice_layout), it splits the keys down the middle and angles each half inward, all on one board. Your wrists sit a little more naturally, and it looks great.

Alice isn't one fixed size either. You'll find Alice boards [from 40% all the way up to full size](https://kbd.news/Keyboard-Form-Factors-Explained-A-Complete-Guide-to-Keyboard-Sizes-115.html).

**Split.** Two separate halves you can place at shoulder width, which [can reduce wrist strain](https://kbd.news/Keyboard-Form-Factors-Explained-A-Complete-Guide-to-Keyboard-Sizes-115.html). Many add thumb keys and tenting, where the halves tilt up in the middle like a little tent.

## So which one?

- **You use spreadsheets or a number pad a lot:** full size or 96%.
- **You game, or just want an easy start:** TKL or 75%.
- **You want a clean desk but still want arrows:** 65%.
- **You travel or love a challenge:** 60% or 40%.
- **Your wrists complain:** look at Alice or split.

Not sure? A 75% is a comfy middle ground. You keep almost every key and still free up space for your mouse.

## What's on my desk

My first keyboard was a Royal Kludge RK65, and it wasn't even hot-swappable. Before that, I'd only used the A4Tech keyboards at computer shops, so playing Dota 2 on a board with no function row took some adjusting. I got there, and 65% is now my favorite layout. Here's where my six boards land:

- **60%:** Tofu60
- **65%:** Lucky65 v1, Nut65, Akko MOD005
- **75%:** Attack Shark X86
- **TKL (80%):** PMO Aurora 80
