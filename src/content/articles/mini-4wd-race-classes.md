You built your first Mini 4WD. It's fast, it's shiny, and you're ready to race. Then the sign-up sheet asks: "Which class?" Box Stock? Stock? B-MAX? Open? Suddenly it feels like a pop quiz nobody warned you about.

Don't worry. A class is just a rulebook that says **how much you're allowed to change your car**. Some classes say "barely anything". Others say "go wild, just keep it Tamiya". Here's each one, from strictest to wildest.

## First, the rules every car follows

Before any class rules kick in, Tamiya's [official race regulations](https://tamiya.com/english/mini4wd/regulation.htm) set the size of the box your car has to fit in:

- **Width:** under 105 mm
- **Height:** under 70 mm
- **Length:** under 165 mm
- **Ground clearance:** at least 1 mm
- **Weight:** at least 90 g, counting the batteries and motor

Tires must be 22 to 35 mm across and 8 to 26 mm wide. Tamiya's rules also say only Tamiya-branded AA batteries, though they note some events set their own battery rules. That's why your local race might allow rechargeables when an official one doesn't. Every class below starts from these limits and adds its own.

## Box Stock: straight out of the box

This is the "no shortcuts" class. Fun twist: Tamiya doesn't actually have a class called Box Stock. As one Malaysian hobby shop [puts it](https://monkeemods.com/tamiya/blog/tamiya-mini-4wd-race-classes-explained), "'Box Stock' is not a Tamiya regulation title." It's a class shops and organisers create, so the details change from place to place.

In the Philippines, the [Box Stock rules from Lil's Hobby Center](https://www.lilshobbycenter.com.ph/blogs/regulations/box-stock-competition-rules-2026), which calls itself the [exclusive Tamiya distributor of Mini 4WD cars](https://www.lilshobbycenter.com.ph/collections/mini-4wd) in the Philippines, look like this:

- One complete kit, built the way the instructions say. No mixing parts from other kits.
- **Only the Tamiya Type 130 motor** (the one with the white cap). If your kit came with a faster tuned motor, swap it out.
- No tune-up or upgrade parts at all.
- Starter Kits and EVO kits aren't allowed.
- Stickers on: plain, naked bodies don't count. Only clear polycarbonate bodies can be painted.

Box Stock is the cheapest way to race, and it's a skill test in disguise. When everyone has the same parts, a clean build wins.

**Heads up:** other places run Box Stock differently. A Las Vegas hobby shop, for example, [allows any silver Mabuchi-style motor and any brand of AA battery](https://www.downtownhobby9.com/box-stock-rules). Always read the rules for *your* race.

## Stock Class: bolt it on, don't cut it

This one *is* official. Tamiya's [Stock Class rules](https://tamiya.com/english/mini4wd/regulation_stockclass.html) (current as of May 11, 2026) let you add upgrade parts, but they keep your tools in the drawer:

- **No cutting or shaving the chassis**, and no new screw holes. You can countersink existing holes, or drill through or widen them to 2 mm.
- **Dash motors are allowed**, but not motors you've taken apart.
- **Plates are fine**, carbon ones included, as long as they're fixed tight and don't wobble. No tapered edges.
- **Rollers** only in the allowed combinations, and really tiny rollers are out.
- **Tires** can't be cut or shaved, but you can mix sizes.
- Clear and polycarbonate bodies are okay. Splitting a body into pieces isn't.

Tamiya Japan [describes Stock Class](https://tamiya.com/japan/event/m4stsc2026_0806_078) as limited part modification, so you can enjoy racing with mostly bolt-on parts. Translation: you'll spend your weekend tuning, not sanding.

## B-MAX: Japan's beginner-friendly rulebook

B-MAX GP isn't made by Tamiya. It's run by the [Basic-MAX GP committee](https://basic-max.com/about/), founded in June 2021 in Yokohama, Japan, with Tamiya listed as a cooperating company. The committee says its rules build on Tamiya's official ones but limit part modifications and gimmicks, to lower the barrier for beginners and returning racers.

Here's the neat part. B-MAX's [latest rules, version 5.0](https://basic-max.com/2026/05/13/bmaxgp-regu-v5/), started on June 1, 2026, the same day as Tamiya's revised Stock Class. They're almost the same: no cutting the chassis, no new screw holes, dash motors okay, carbon plates okay. B-MAX adds a few picky extras. If a roller or mass damper hole wears down enough, it counts as a modification. In plain words, a car built for one class can usually run the other.

We couldn't find B-MAX races in the Philippines yet. If your track runs one, tell us!

## Open Class: anything Tamiya, anything goes

This is where cars start looking like tiny spaceships. In Open, you race under Tamiya's [full official regulations](https://tamiya.com/english/mini4wd/regulation.htm):

- **You can cut away plastic and cut holes in the chassis.** Homemade chassis are still banned.
- Every part has to come from Tamiya's Mini 4WD, R/C Mini 4WD or Dangun Racer lines. Parts can be trimmed or drilled, but they still need to be recognisable.
- Carbon, FRP and metal parts can't be reshaped. Gears can be drilled or lightened, as long as they keep their gear ratios.
- Tamiya motors only, from an approved list, and no rewinding the coils.

At Tamiya's Japan Cup, the "Open Class" is actually [an age group](https://www.tamiya.com/japan/mini4wd/japancup.html) (high school age and up) racing under those same official rules. In the Philippines, the [Open rules from Lil's Hobby Center](https://www.lilshobbycenter.com.ph/blogs/regulations/open-competition-regulations-2026) follow Tamiya's official limits too, and allow untampered rechargeable batteries at local races.

Open is where the fastest cars live, and also the fastest-emptying wallets. You've been warned.

## What about the other Philippine classes?

Local races love new classes. Lil's Hobby Center's 2026 lineup includes [Open, xStock, Box Stock, Stock Advance, Ultra Dyipne and TRF Max](https://www.lilshobbycenter.com.ph/blogs/regulations/open-competition-regulations-2026). Two you'll hear a lot:

- **[Stock Advance](https://www.lilshobbycenter.com.ph/pages/mini4wd-stock-advance-rules-and-regulations-2025):** no modifying any part, up to 3 FRP plates, unlimited Tamiya plastic rollers, no carbon, aluminium or bearings, and only the Torque-, Rev- or Atomic-Tuned 2 motors (or their PRO versions).
- **[xStock](https://www.lilshobbycenter.com.ph/pages/phcup-2026-xstock-class-regulations):** a PH Cup class with its own rulebook. The [first leg of PH Cup 2025](https://cinemabravo.com/2025/03/05/tamiya-mini-4wd-philippine-cup-2025-kicks-off-winners-set-for-taiwan-and-japan/) drew over 300 racers in xStock on day one alone.

## So which one should you race first?

- **Brand new?** Start with **Box Stock**. It's cheap and fair, and you learn how your car really behaves.
- **Caught the bug?** Move to **Stock** (or B-MAX if you're in Japan). Upgrades make a real difference, and you still don't need a rotary tool.
- **Can't stop thinking about it at 2am?** Welcome to **Open**. You live here now.

Whatever you pick, the golden rule is the same: **read the organiser's rules before race day**, and keep your car legal for the inspection table. Nothing hurts like a fast car that isn't allowed on the track.
