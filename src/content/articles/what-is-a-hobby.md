When I was a kid, my mom brought home a new toy every month, right after payday. I didn't see it as a gift back then. I saw it as *the schedule*.

That schedule built a collection. Dragon Ball Z figures. Spider-Man and the Green Goblin from the first Spider-Man movie, the Bump N Go kind. Then came Crush Gear, Zoids, Beyblades, and a yo-yo I mastered exactly two tricks on.

Nobody sat me down and said "congratulations, you have a hobby." It just happened. So let's answer the question properly.

## So, what is a hobby?

Merriam-Webster defines a hobby as "a pursuit outside one's regular occupation engaged in especially for relaxation."

In plain words: a hobby is the thing you do because you *want* to. No boss, no grades, no paycheck. Just you, the thing you love, and the promise of "five more minutes" that has never once been five minutes.

## Fun fact: the word started as a horse

Around the year 1400, a "hobby" was a small, active horse. By the 1550s, a "hobbyhorse" was a pretend horse used in morris dancing, and by the 1580s it was a child's toy horse. In the 1670s, people started using "hobbyhorse" to mean a favorite pastime, and by 1816 plain old "hobby" meant it too.

So technically, every hobby is riding a toy horse very seriously. Honestly, that explains a lot about grown adults lubing keyboard switches at 2am.

Bonus: "hobby" is also the name of a small falcon. Hobbies really can take off.

## What counts as a hobby?

If you've ever said "just one more," it counts. Most hobbies fall into one of three camps, which is where this site gets its name:

![Four hobby scenes: a figure display case and One Piece card packs at Eclipse Lab, two Beyblade X tops in a stadium, and a Mini 4WD track at SM Store](/images/articles/what-is-a-hobby/collect-play-race.webp)
*Photo: Echo*

- **Build:** putting something together with your own hands, like a [Tamiya](/tamiya) Mini 4WD, a custom [keyboard](/keyboards), or a model kit.
- **Play:** battling, racing, competing. Card games at a [TCG](/tcg) night, ranked matches in [esports](/esports), spinning tops in a [Beyblade](/beyblade) stadium. (Mine was a metal basin from the kitchen. Official stadium? Never heard of her.)
- **Collect:** figures, cards, retro [consoles](/consoles), sneakers, stamps. Anything you line up on a shelf and stare at proudly.

Plenty of hobbies are all three at once. Ask anyone who builds a Mini 4WD, races it every weekend, and then buys a second one "just for display."

## A hobby grows with you

Mine never really left. It just changed outfits.

📷 PHOTO: Your old childhood toys next to what you collect now.

The toys turned into a Game Boy Advance. My very first game was a Pokémon ROM hack from a game shop in the mall (little me had no idea what a ROM hack was, it just had Pokémon in it). Then came a Super Mario Bros. game and Pokémon Ruby.

Then my parents bought me a PSP, and I never saw the outside world again. Not that I had much of a social life in elementary school anyway. That's where I fell in love with Monster Hunter.

Fast forward to PC gaming: Dota 2, TFT, Valorant, and a long list of soulslikes and roguelikes that taught me to enjoy losing on purpose.

And then, Beyblade came back. I bought a Hells Scythe as a gift for my little brother and told myself I'd get one for me next time. Next time, there were none. Not in one mall. Not in *any* mall. I'm not exaggerating. How I finally got mine is a story for another article.

## Why have a hobby?

Here's what mine gave me:

- **Something that's all yours.** Nobody grades how well you collect Zoids.
- **People.** My cousin, my little brother, and every friend I've battled, raced or queued with. Fair warning: hobbies come with a little jealousy. I had a Shield Liger, my cousin had a Blade Liger, and I was convinced his was cooler. Looking back, I should have just been grateful for the awesome toy in my hands.
- **A way back.** Hobbies wait for you. Years later, one Beyblade gift pulled me right back in.

## How to pick your next hobby

1. **Start small.** One starter set, one deck, one kit. You don't need the whole shelf on day one.
2. **Ask yourself: build, play or collect?** Pick the one that sounds the most fun right now.
3. **Find your people.** A local shop, a group chat, a race track, a game night. Hobbies are better with company.
4. **Give it a month.** Some hobbies click on day one. Others sneak up on you.
5. **Don't compare your Shield Liger to someone else's Blade Liger.** Trust me.
